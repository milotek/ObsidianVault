I've thought off-and-on about what to write here. I wasn't popular at
Google, so what I say is unlikely to affect many people. On the other hand,
I have deep thoughts about this that I want to express.

I took the voluntary exit program. I had promised some people who got laid
off in the first round that I'd go down with the ship, that I wanted to see
what it looked like for a division to disintegrate in a company like
Google. I think I mostly held to that commitment, though clearly I could
have stayed until I too was laid off rather than volunteer. I feel a bit
bad for that.

I'm deeply disappointed with Google. That's clearly silly because I'm
personifying an international corporation. I'm going with it though. Google
makes so much money. It had founders with a controlling stake. It had a
strong reputation for engineering and building impossible things.

It has abandoned all of that.

I thought for a time that Larry and Sergei had abandoned Google. They
haven't, they just disagree with me on what it should be. It was the
greatest hope I had for a company that could make money and still hold to
some values I have in common.

Google values nothing but continued growth.

I built nothing of consequence or substance while I was at Google. I met
people, I learned things, I built things, but nothing I did made any
difference, not even to me. I had hoped before coming here that Google had
some secret sauce for engineering excellence, something in the culture or
the processes or the existing technology.

The only "secret sauce" is the firehose of revenue from ads through search
and the leveraging of a monopoly position. This secret sauce gave some
people latitude to do research and briefly dabble in unprofitable things.
It also serves as magical pixie dust that allows management to frequently
be inept and strategy to be incoherent or non-existent for long stretches
without anyone being penalized.

Google is not special. It is just a company that briefly tried some things
and then gave up and became conventional, one short-sighted decision at a
time.

I'm so disappointed.

At least I get to leave.

I think I've finally nailed down what I'm disappointed in. I'm going to put the people at Google into two categories. The Mormon boy in me wants to
call them "believers" and "apostates". I like this because it has a rather
vicious emotional valence. The athiest in me is concerned that using those
as categories will trigger a bunch of unpleasant type-1 thinking I should
avoid. Instead let's call them "utopians" and "pragmatists".

Technology has always had a certain amount of appeal to people who are
Utopian by nature. I honestly don't know what about Google made it special
in attracting people with this mindset. I do think that the broad latitude
it gave workers strengthened the attraction.

Pragmatists are just attracted by money, power, and prestige. Google
provides these in spades, especially if its all you value. Pragmatists are
generally willing to support diversity, or covering up sexual harrassment,
mental health, contributing to any political party, or building AI to help
kill people. You have to be pragmatic. It's just business.

I'm a Utopian. I want to build things that improve the future. I'm also a
Material Humanist. I think that modern western democracy owes much of its
incredible growth to the changes outlined in "The WEIRDest People in the
World" by Joseph Henrich. Specifically, the shift from tribalism to
individualism and abstract rules brought about huge changes in what
humanity is capable of.

I believe there is a second major shift going on, and that Utopians, as
represented by many Googlers I've worked with, are harbingers of that
second shift. It's a shift from people being part of a community, city,
state, or nation that is geographically and culturally distinct. Instead
people curate highly connected networks and long-running identities to
create organizations that are far more complex and faster-moving than
states.

Google, Meta, Apple, Microsoft, and Amazon are each doing work to profit
from this shift. The business models they use are broadly harmful, slowing
things down, and diverting benefits away from individuals and away from the
organizations they wish to contribute towards, and instead toward
technological gate-keepers and rent-seekers. This is exacerbated by the Rot
Economy as discussed by Ed Zitron.

I think the network will route around this damage eventually.

I don't know how long it will take.

I've been working in technology for 30 years. We don't need more chat
protocols. We do need improvements to email. We don't need AI chatbots. We
do need to liberate our personal data from our service providers. We don't
need more short-form videos. We need to empower parents to protect their
children from the various business people selling their attention.

There are so many, many problems I thought we as technologist would truly
and finally solve by now. We've made very little progress on them. That's
because it's far more profitable to continue selling ineffective solutions
than it is to solve problems.

I'd much rather work toward creating actual solutions that enable humanity
to have a better future rather than making myself even richer by continuing
to be latched on to Google

I don't know what I'll do next.

But I know I'd rather start looking than stay here.
